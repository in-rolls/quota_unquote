\begin{abstract}
\noindent The linked Uttar Pradesh sample shows no improvement in measured Good Governance under women's reservations. The frozen PAI 2.0 specification yields a conditional difference of \UpPaiTwoDifference\ points (95\% CI: \UpPaiTwoCILow, \UpPaiTwoCIHigh), or \UpPaiTwoDifferenceSD\ control-group standard deviations (95\% CI: \UpPaiTwoCILowSD, \UpPaiTwoCIHighSD). An exploratory Rajasthan analysis also yields a near-zero estimate. The assignment mechanisms remain unverified, so these are conditional associations. PAI measures reported governance procedure and performance, not corruption.
\end{abstract}

Women's reservations put women in office, but the UP and Rajasthan estimates show no improvement in measured Good Governance. Political reservations can change local government only when elected women exercise authority. A woman sarpanch may curb leakage, open decisions to public scrutiny, or improve administrative procedure. These outcomes are not interchangeable. A clean audit directly bears on corruption; a meeting recorded in an administrative portal bears on procedure.

This paper keeps those measures separate. Evidence for a corruption claim must come from program leakage and social audits. The Panchayat Advancement Index, or PAI, measures governance and reported performance across Gram Panchayats. Its Good Governance theme covers accounts, public meetings, beneficiary lists, grievance systems, procurement, planning, and online administration. A higher score may reflect more accountable government, better record keeping, or greater effort to satisfy the index. It does not by itself show that graft fell.

\section{Research design}

The frozen UP analysis compares Gram Panchayats with a women-reserved pradhan seat in 2021 with Gram Panchayats in a known non-women category in the same district, LGD block, and caste-reservation class. The exploratory Rajasthan analysis makes the analogous 2020 comparison within Panchayat Samiti and caste-reservation strata. Each coefficient is an unweighted difference with assignment-stratum fixed effects.

The causal interpretation requires the official reservation allocation to be random within the reconstructed strata. The governing laws establish reservation and rotation, but the current evidence does not establish random allocation within these strata. The estimates therefore describe conditional associations. Randomization inference shows what the test would yield under a fixed-count assignment mechanism; it does not prove that officials used that mechanism.

The primary outcome is the PAI 2.0 Good Governance score for fiscal year 2023/24. The unit and weight are one Gram Panchayat and one vote in the average. PAI 1.0 is a separate replication because its indicators and scoring system differ from PAI 2.0. The two versions are not a panel outcome.

\section{Measurement and linkage}

UP election names, reservations, winner sex, and LGD links are standardized once in the \texttt{local\_elections\_up} repository. The analysis imports that pinned release rather than maintaining a second set of name repairs. The UP primary analysis spans \UpPaiTwoBlocks\ assignment blocks. A manually checked score-stratified sample supports the accepted election-to-LGD threshold.

PAI 1.0 reports LGD Gram Panchayat codes, which permit a direct join. PAI 2.0 omits those codes. In UP, the analysis links exact normalized official GP names within district and LGD block, then tries exact election names for the remaining rows. In Rajasthan, the linkage also maps old district and block groups to the post-reorganization PAI geography through a reviewed crosswalk.

The matcher never decides a reviewed analysis link. It writes candidate pairs, scores, and one-to-one proposals to an audit directory. Reviewers inspect those pairs without reservation status or PAI scores and move only approved links into the active crosswalk. A failed link remains missing rather than becoming a zero. In UP PAI 2.0, coverage is \UpPaiTwoWomenMatchPct\% for women-reserved seats and \UpPaiTwoOpenMatchPct\% for other seats.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\section{UP results}

The UP estimate is near zero and precise. The primary linked sample contains \UpPaiTwoN\ Gram Panchayats in \UpPaiTwoStrata\ informative strata. The conditional difference is \UpPaiTwoDifference\ PAI 2.0 points (95\% CI: \UpPaiTwoCILow, \UpPaiTwoCIHigh), or \UpPaiTwoDifferenceSD\ control-group standard deviations (95\% CI: \UpPaiTwoCILowSD, \UpPaiTwoCIHighSD). The upper bound rules out improvements larger than about \UpPaiTwoCIHighSD\ standard deviations in this linked sample. The fixed-count randomization sensitivity gives $p=\UpPaiTwoRIP$.

The PAI 1.0 replication agrees. Its conditional difference is \UpPaiOneDifference\ points (95\% CI: \UpPaiOneCILow, \UpPaiOneCIHigh), or \UpPaiOneDifferenceSD\ standard deviations (95\% CI: \UpPaiOneCILowSD, \UpPaiOneCIHighSD). Restricting PAI 2.0 to the \UpPaiTwoExactN\ GPs linked by exact election-to-LGD names yields \UpPaiTwoExactDifference\ points (95\% CI: \UpPaiTwoExactCILow, \UpPaiTwoExactCIHigh). The block-clustered CR2 interval is similarly narrow (\UpPaiTwoCRTwoCILow, \UpPaiTwoCRTwoCIHigh\ points).

\section{Scope of the claim}

The evidence supports a narrow result: women-reserved seats are not associated with better PAI Good Governance in the linked samples. The UP interval excludes even a small positive association on the standardized scale. The evidence does not yet identify a causal effect because the assignment mechanisms remain unverified, and incomplete portal coverage restricts the population. It cannot show that women pradhans reduce corruption because PAI does not measure leakage, bribery, or graft.
